## 1. Compound Identification

**Common name:** Magnesium L-threonate

**Trade name:** Magtein®

**Chemical class:** Magnesium salt of L-threonic acid (a naturally occurring metabolite of vitamin C / ascorbic acid)

**IUPAC name:** Magnesium (2R,3S)-2,3,4-trihydroxybutanoate

**CAS Registry Number:** 778571-57-6

**Molecular formula:** C₈H₁₄MgO₁₀

**Molecular weight:** 294.50 g/mol

**Elemental magnesium content:** Approximately 7.2% by weight (approximately 144 mg elemental Mg per 2,000 mg MgT)

**Physical properties:** White to off-white crystalline powder; freely soluble in water; stable under standard storage conditions.

**Origin:** Magnesium L-threonate was developed by Guosong Liu, MD, PhD, and collaborators at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) and Tsinghua University, Beijing, China. The compound was designed to deliver magnesium across the blood-brain barrier (BBB) more effectively than conventional magnesium salts (oxide, citrate, glycinate, etc.), leveraging the natural presence of L-threonate in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and its active transport via glucose transporters (GLUTs) on neuronal membranes.

**Regulatory status:** Magnesium L-threonate is sold as an over-the-counter (OTC) dietary supplement in the United States under the Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act (DSHEA) of 1994. It has self-affirmed Generally Recognized as Safe (GRAS) status. It is not currently approved by the FDA as a drug for the treatment of cognitive decline or any neurological disorder.

**Key distinguishing feature:** Unlike other magnesium salts, MgT significantly elevates brain magnesium levels and cerebrospinal fluid magnesium concentration following oral administration. The threonate anion, rather than the magnesium cation alone, is the critical mediator of intraneuronal magnesium elevation — threonate enters neurons via GLUTs and directly increases intracellular Mg²⁺ concentration. Other common magnesium anions (citrate, glycinate, taurate, oxide) do not replicate this effect on brain magnesium.

## 2. Therapeutic Application

**Primary indication disclosed:** Enhancement of synaptic density and cognitive function in age-related cognitive decline.

**Target outcomes:**

- **Overall cognitive performance improvement:** In a 2026 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (n=100, 6 weeks, 2 g/day Magtein®), the MgT group showed significantly greater improvement on the NIH Cognitive Toolbox Total Cognition Composite score compared to placebo (p=0.043).

- **Working and episodic memory enhancement:** The same trial demonstrated larger treatment effects on working memory and episodic memory domains.

- **Brain cognitive age reduction:** MgT treatment was associated with a 7.5-year reduction in estimated brain cognitive age based on NIH Toolbox normative data.

- **Reaction time improvement:** Significant improvement in reaction time in the MgT group versus placebo (p=0.031).

- **Executive function restoration:** A 2016 trial in older adults (50–70 years, n=44, 12 weeks) demonstrated that MMFS-01 (MgT) significantly improved overall cognitive ability (p=0.003, Cohen's d=0.91) and nearly restored impaired executive function to age-matched normative levels.

- **Autonomic balance improvement:** MgT supplementation was associated with greater reduction in resting heart rate (p=0.030) and increased heart rate variability (p=0.036), indicating improved parasympathetic tone and stress reduction.

**Patient population:** Adults experiencing age-related cognitive decline, including subjective cognitive complaints, mild cognitive impairment (MCI), and age-associated memory impairment. Clinical trials have enrolled adults aged 18–70, with the most therapeutically relevant populations being adults aged 50 and older with demonstrable cognitive impairment or age-related cognitive slowing.

**Secondary indications supported by preclinical evidence:**

- Alzheimer's disease-related cognitive deficits (demonstrated in APPswe/PS1dE9 transgenic mouse models)
- Anxiety-related cognitive impairment (enhanced fear extinction in prefrontal cortex without erasing original fear memory)
- Sleep-related cognitive impairment (improvement in sleep-related impairment scores, p=0.043, in participants with more severe sleep disturbance)

## 3. Proposed Protocol

**Compound:** Magnesium L-threonate (CAS 778571-57-6), oral capsule or powder form.

**Dosage:** 2,000 mg (2 g) magnesium L-threonate per day, providing approximately 144 mg elemental magnesium. Published clinical trials have used daily doses of 1,500–2,000 mg MgT.

**Dosing schedule:** The total daily dose is typically divided into two administrations: approximately 1,000 mg in the morning and 1,000 mg in the evening (or approximately 667 mg TID as used in some protocols). Some protocols administer the full evening dose before sleep, based on the compound's concurrent effects on sleep quality.

**Treatment duration:** Clinical benefits have been demonstrated at 6 weeks (2026 Frontiers in Nutrition trial) and 12 weeks (2016 Journal of Alzheimer's Disease trial). A minimum of 6 weeks of continuous supplementation is recommended based on available evidence, with 12 weeks being the standard duration for full cognitive assessment.

**Monitoring:** Cognitive function should be assessed at baseline and at the end of the supplementation period using validated instruments such as the NIH Cognitive Toolbox, Trail Making Test (TMT-A and TMT-B for executive function), or equivalent standardized cognitive batteries. Sleep quality monitoring (e.g., PROMIS Sleep-Related Impairment) and cardiovascular parameters (resting heart rate, heart rate variability) may also be assessed.

**Safety considerations:** Magnesium L-threonate is generally well tolerated. The 2026 Frontiers in Nutrition trial reported no significant adverse reactions compared to placebo. Common side effects of oral magnesium supplementation in general include gastrointestinal effects (loose stools, mild diarrhea) at high doses, but MgT at the recommended dosage delivers a relatively modest elemental magnesium load (144 mg, well below the 350 mg/day tolerable upper intake level for supplemental magnesium set by the Institute of Medicine). Patients with chronic kidney disease or severe renal impairment should use magnesium supplements only under medical supervision due to the risk of hypermagnesemia. No significant drug interactions specific to MgT have been identified, though general magnesium supplementation may reduce absorption of certain antibiotics (tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones) and bisphosphonates if taken concurrently.

## 4. Mechanism of Action

Magnesium L-threonate enhances synaptic density and cognitive function through a well-characterized multi-step molecular cascade:

**Step 1: Oral absorption and CNS penetration.** Oral MgT is absorbed in the gastrointestinal tract. The L-threonate anion crosses the blood-brain barrier and enters cerebrospinal fluid, where it is naturally present at physiological concentrations. Oral MgT administration demonstrably elevates CSF threonate levels in preclinical models (Sun et al., 2016, Neuropharmacology, PMID: 27178134).

**Step 2: GLUT-mediated neuronal threonate uptake.** Once in the extracellular CNS compartment, L-threonate enters neurons through glucose transporters (GLUTs). This uptake mechanism is specific to threonate — other common magnesium anions do not utilize this transport pathway and therefore cannot replicate the intraneuronal magnesium elevation produced by MgT. This specificity was demonstrated by Sun et al. (2016): in cultured hippocampal neurons, only threonate treatment (not other magnesium anions) increased intracellular Mg²⁺ concentration.

**Step 3: Intraneuronal Mg²⁺ elevation.** Threonate entry into neurons directly raises intracellular magnesium concentration ([Mg²⁺]ᵢ). This elevation is the critical mechanistic step distinguishing MgT from other magnesium supplements and is the proximate cause of the downstream synaptic effects.

**Step 4: Presynaptic terminal reconfiguration.** Elevated [Mg²⁺]ᵢ increases the number of functional presynaptic release sites (as measured by synaptophysin- and synaptobrevin-positive puncta density) while simultaneously reducing release probability at individual sites. Zhou et al. (2024, Nature Communications) reported that presynaptic intracellular Mg²⁺ shifts synapses on hippocampal dendritic branches between a low-transmission-efficiency, high-plasticity configuration (favouring information encoding) and the inverse configuration (favouring storage), and that elevating brain Mg²⁺ in aging animals restored a young-like synaptic configuration alongside improved learning and memory.

**Step 5: NR2B-containing NMDA receptor upregulation.** Elevated extracellular and intracellular magnesium upregulates expression of NR2B subunit-containing NMDA receptors at postsynaptic densities in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex. NR2B-containing NMDARs have longer current decay times than NR2A-containing receptors, providing a wider temporal window for coincidence detection — the biophysical basis of associative learning and memory. This upregulation was demonstrated in vivo by Slutsky et al. (2010) and confirmed in vitro by Sun et al. (2016).

**Step 6: Enhanced long-term potentiation (LTP).** The combination of increased presynaptic release site density and enhanced postsynaptic NMDAR current produces robust enhancement of long-term potentiation — the cellular correlate of learning and memory. Both early-phase LTP (protein synthesis-independent) and late-phase LTP (protein synthesis-dependent) are enhanced by MgT treatment.

**Step 7: CREB/BDNF signaling activation.** Enhanced NMDAR signaling activates the CREB (cAMP response element-binding protein) transcription factor, which in turn upregulates expression of BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor). BDNF is a critical neurotrophin for synaptic growth, dendritic spine formation, and neuronal survival. Abumaria et al. (2011, J. Neuroscience) confirmed increased BDNF expression in the prefrontal cortex following MgT treatment.

**Step 8: Structural and functional synapse density increase.** The cumulative effect of steps 4–7 is a measurable increase in both structural synapse density (electron microscopy-confirmed synapse counts) and functional synapse density (electrophysiologically active synaptic connections) in the hippocampal DG and CA1 subregions and in the prefrontal cortex. This increase in synaptic density is the morphological substrate for improved learning, memory consolidation, and cognitive flexibility.

**Step 9: Mitochondrial membrane potential enhancement.** Threonate treatment additionally boosts mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) in neurons (Sun et al., 2016), supporting the increased energy demands of enhanced synaptic activity and providing neuroprotective benefit.

**Step 10 (in Alzheimer's disease models): Amyloid-beta pathway modulation.** In APPswe/PS1dE9 transgenic mice, MgT treatment prevented upregulation of BACE1 (β-secretase), reduced soluble APPβ (sAPPβ) and β-C-terminal fragment (β-CTF) levels, reduced amyloid-beta plaque burden, and prevented calcineurin overactivation that would otherwise reduce synaptic NMDAR expression (Li et al., 2014, Molecular Brain, PMID: 25213836). These effects were observed even when MgT was administered at late stages of disease progression.

## 5. Evidence Summary

**5.1 Seminal preclinical study: Slutsky et al. (2010)**

**Citation:** Slutsky I, Abumaria N, Wu LJ, Huang C, Zhang L, Li B, et al. Enhancement of Learning and Memory by Elevating Brain Magnesium. *Neuron*. 2010;65(2):165-177. doi:10.1016/j.neuron.2009.12.026. PMID: 20152124.

This five-year study at MIT and Tsinghua University established the foundational evidence for MgT:

| Outcome | Finding |
|---------|---------|
| Young rat learning | MgT enhanced learning abilities, working memory, and short- and long-term memory |
| Aged rat cognition | MgT improved pattern completion ability (a hippocampus-dependent cognitive function that declines with aging) |
| Synaptic density | Higher density of synaptophysin-/synaptobrevin-positive puncta in hippocampal DG and CA1 regions |
| Synaptic mechanism | Increased functional presynaptic release sites with reduced per-site release probability |
| NMDAR expression | Upregulation of NR2B-containing NMDA receptors |
| LTP | Enhanced short-term synaptic facilitation and long-term potentiation |
| Brain Mg²⁺ | MgT, unlike MgCl₂ diet, significantly elevated brain magnesium concentrations |

**5.2 Fear extinction and prefrontal cortex plasticity: Abumaria et al. (2011)**

**Citation:** Abumaria N, Yin B, Zhang L, Li XY, Chen T, et al. Neuroscience*. 2011;31(42):14871-14881. doi:10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3782-11.2011. PMID: 22016520. PMCID: PMC6623582.

| Outcome | Finding |
|---------|---------|
| Fear extinction | MgT enhanced retention of extinction memory without erasing original fear memory |
| Regional specificity | Enhanced NMDAR signaling and BDNF expression in prefrontal cortex but not amygdala |
| Synaptic plasticity | Increased presynaptic puncta density and LTP selectively in infralimbic PFC |

**5.3 Alzheimer's disease model: Li et al. (2014)**

**Citation:** Li W, Yu J, Liu Y, Huang X, Abumaria N, Zhu Y, et al. Elevation of brain magnesium prevents synaptic loss and reverses cognitive deficits in Alzheimer's disease mouse model. *Molecular Brain*. 2014;7:65. doi:10.1186/s13041-014-0065-y. PMID: 25213836. PMCID: PMC4172865.

| Outcome | Finding |

2. (2010, *Neuron*). The mechanistic pathway and therapeutic application are thoroughly described in the public scientific literature.

3. The patents primarily claim the *composition* (magnesium L-threonate and formulations thereof) and its *use for* cognitive and neurological indications. 4. Magnesium L-threonate itself (CAS 778571-57-6) is a defined chemical compound that can be synthesized by any qualified chemical manufacturer. Multiple suppliers offer MgT as a bulk ingredient. The compound patent claims are directed at compositions and uses, not at the chemical entity per se.

## 8. Limitations and Disclaimer

1. **Not medical advice.** This document is a defensive prior-art disclosure intended to document known therapeutic uses of magnesium L-threonate in the public domain. It is not medical advice, a treatment recommendation, or a clinical guideline. Patients should consult qualified healthcare providers before initiating any supplement regimen, particularly those with existing neurological conditions.

2. **Regulatory status.** Magnesium L-threonate is classified as a dietary supplement in the United States and is not FDA-approved for the treatment of age-related cognitive decline, Alzheimer's disease, or any neurological disorder. The evidence summarized here is drawn from published clinical trials and preclinical studies and may not meet the evidentiary standards required for regulatory drug approval.

3. **Clinical trial limitations.** The two human RCTs (Liu et al., 2016, n=44; Frontiers in Nutrition, 2026, n=100) represent relatively small sample sizes. The 2016 trial enrolled participants aged 50–70 with existing cognitive impairment, while the 2026 trial enrolled younger adults (18–45) with sleep complaints. Larger, multi-center trials with longer treatment durations and diverse populations are needed to fully establish the clinical utility of MgT for age-related cognitive decline.

4. **Preclinical-to-clinical translation.** Much of the mechanistic evidence (synaptic density quantification, NMDAR expression changes, LTP enhancement, Alzheimer's model data) comes from rodent studies. While the human clinical trials demonstrate cognitive benefit, direct confirmation of the proposed synaptic mechanism in human brain tissue has not been performed.

5. **Specificity of cognitive domains.** MgT showed significant improvements in working memory, episodic memory, and reaction time, but did not improve fluid reasoning (Raven's Progressive Matrices, p=0.953 in the 2026 trial). The cognitive benefits may be domain-specific rather than global.

6. **Conflict of interest note.** Several key studies were conducted by researchers affiliated with Magceutics, Inc. / Neurocentria, Inc., the holders of the core MgT patents. Independent replication by groups without commercial ties to MgT would strengthen the evidence base.

7. **Long-term safety and efficacy.** The longest controlled human trial was 12 weeks. Long-term safety and sustained cognitive benefit beyond 12 weeks have not been established in controlled trials.

8. **Renal considerations.** Patients with moderate to severe chronic kidney disease (eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73m²) are at risk of magnesium accumulation and should not take MgT or any magnesium supplement without nephrology guidance.

9. **Patent landscape completeness.** The patent survey in Section 6 is based on publicly available patent databases as of April 2026 and may not capture all pending applications, continuations, divisionals, or recently granted patents in all jurisdictions. This disclosure is intended to supplement, not replace, formal freedom-to-operate analyses.
