## 2. Therapeutic Application

Cellular senescence accumulates with age and in response to DNA damage, oxidative stress, telomere attrition, oncogene activation, and mitochondrial dysfunction. Senescent cells resist apoptosis through upregulation of senescent cell anti-apoptotic pathways (SCAPs), including the BCL-2/BCL-xL family, PI3K/AKT, p53/p21/serpine, the receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) network, and HIF-1α signaling. Although representing a small fraction of total tissue cellularity, senescent cells secrete the SASP — a cocktail of pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1α, IL-6, IL-8, TNF-α), matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), and growth factors — that drives paracrine senescence in neighboring cells, chronic sterile inflammation, tissue fibrosis, and organ dysfunction.

The dasatinib + quercetin (D+Q) combination selectively targets these SCAP networks to induce apoptosis in senescent cells while sparing quiescent and proliferating cells. Published preclinical and clinical evidence supports the therapeutic relevance of D+Q senolytic therapy in:

- **Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF):** Senescent cell accumulation in fibrotic lung tissue contributes to progressive, irreversible fibrosis. D+Q reduced senescent cell markers and improved physical function in IPF patients (Justice et al. 2019).
- **Diabetic kidney disease (DKD):** Senescent cells in glomerular and tubular compartments drive renal inflammation and fibrosis. D+Q decreased circulating SASP factors, adipose tissue senescent cell burden, and skin epidermal p16^INK4a+ cell counts in DKD patients (Hickson et al. 2019).
- **Cardiovascular dysfunction:** A single dose of D+Q improved cardiac ejection fraction and carotid vascular reactivity in aged mice within 5 days (Zhu et al. 2015).
- **Age-related physical decline and frailty:** Intermittent D+Q extended healthspan and delayed age-related symptoms including osteoporosis, vertebral disk degeneration, and gait impairment in progeroid mouse models (Zhu et al. 2015).
- **Intestinal senescence and dysbiosis:** D+Q alleviates intestinal senescence, reduces inflammation markers, and modulates the gut microbiome composition in aged mice (Saccon et al. 2021).

## 3. Proposed Protocol

The following dosing protocol is drawn directly from published clinical trials and represents the regimen used in the first-in-human studies of D+Q as a senolytic combination:

| Parameter | Specification | Source |
|-----------|--------------|--------|
| **Dasatinib dose** | 100 mg/day oral | Justice et al. 2019; Hickson et al. 2019 |
| **Quercetin dose** | 1000–1250 mg/day oral | Justice et al. 2019 (1250 mg); Hickson et al. 2019 (1000 mg) |
| **Dosing schedule** | Intermittent: 3 consecutive days per treatment course | Justice et al. 2019; Hickson et al. 2019 |
| **Cycle frequency** | Varies by indication: weekly for 3 weeks (IPF trial) or 3 days on then off for multiple cycles | Justice et al. 2019; Hickson et al. 2019 |
| **Route** | Oral administration | Both trials |
| **Treatment setting** | Outpatient; no hospitalization required | Both trials |

The intermittent dosing strategy is pharmacologically rational because senolytic action requires only transient disruption of SCAP networks to trigger apoptosis in senescent cells. Continuous exposure is unnecessary and would increase side-effect risk from dasatinib's broader kinase inhibition profile. The "hit-and-run" model means that senescent cells, once pushed past the apoptotic threshold, are cleared by phagocytes, and the benefit persists for weeks to months even after drug washout — as demonstrated by the 7-month sustained improvement in exercise capacity after a single dose in irradiated mice (Zhu et al. 2015).

## 4. Mechanism of Action

The D+Q combination achieves broader senolytic coverage than either agent alone because dasatinib and quercetin target complementary nodes in the SCAP network:

**Dasatinib** is a multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor originally developed for BCR-ABL in chronic myeloid leukemia. In the senolytic context, dasatinib acts primarily through:

- **SRC family kinase inhibition:** SRC, YES, FYN kinases are upregulated in senescent preadipocytes and signal through focal adhesion kinase (FAK) to promote survival. Dasatinib inhibits this pathway, removing a critical anti-apoptotic signal.
- **Ephrin receptor blockade:** Ephrin-dependent signaling (EFNB1, EFNB3, EPHA2) is upregulated in senescent cells and contributes to resistance to anoikis and apoptosis. Dasatinib suppresses ephrin receptor autophosphorylation.
- **PDGFR and c-KIT inhibition:** Broader receptor tyrosine kinase suppression reduces redundant survival signaling.

Dasatinib is most effective against senescent human fat cell progenitors (preadipocytes) and less effective against senescent endothelial cells when used alone.

**Quercetin** is a flavonol that inhibits a distinct set of SCAP nodes:

- **PI3K inhibition:** Quercetin directly inhibits phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K), reducing downstream AKT phosphorylation and thereby suppressing the PI3K/AKT pro-survival axis.
- **BCL-2 family modulation:** By reducing AKT-mediated phosphorylation of BAD and BAX, quercetin shifts the BCL-2/BAX ratio toward pro-apoptotic, lowering the threshold for mitochondrial outer membrane permeabilization (MOMP) and caspase activation.
- **Serpine (PAI-2) pathway disruption:** Quercetin downregulates plasminogen activator inhibitor-2 and related serpins that protect senescent cells from urokinase-mediated apoptosis.
- **HIF-1α suppression:** Quercetin inhibits hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha signaling, which senescent cells exploit for metabolic adaptation and survival under stress.

Quercetin is most effective against senescent human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs) and senescent mouse bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (BM-MSCs) when used alone.

**Combination rationale:** Because different cell types rely on different SCAP nodes for survival, the D+Q combination achieves complementary coverage. Dasatinib removes tyrosine kinase-dependent survival signaling (dominant in senescent preadipocytes), while quercetin removes PI3K/AKT/BCL-2-dependent survival signaling (dominant in senescent endothelial and mesenchymal cells). Together, they reduce senescent cell viability across multiple tissue compartments simultaneously. This was demonstrated in the original Zhu et al. 2015 study, where the combination eliminated senescent cells more broadly than either drug alone.

## 5. Evidence Summary

### 5.1 Zhu et al. 2015 — Discovery of D+Q as First Senolytic Combination

**Citation:** Zhu Y, Tchkonia T, Pirtskhalava T, Gower AC, Ding H, Giorgadze N, Palmer AK, Ikeno Y, Hubbard GB, Lenburg M, O'Hara SP, LaRusso NF, Miller JD, Roos CM, Verzosa GC, LeBrasseur NK, Wren JD, Farr JN, Khosla S, Stout MB, McGowan SJ, Fuhrmann-Stroissnigg H, Gurkar AU, Zhao J, Colangelo D, Dorronsoro A, Ling YY, Barghouthy AS, Navarro DC, Sano T, Robbins PD, Niedernhofer LJ, Kirkland JL. The Achilles' heel of senescent cells: from transcriptome to senolytic drugs. *Aging Cell*. 2015;14(4):644-658. doi:10.1111/acel.12344. PMID: 25754370.

**Design:** Bioinformatic transcriptome analysis of senescent vs. non-senescent human preadipocytes to identify pro-survival (SCAP) networks, followed by targeted drug screening and in vivo validation in aged and progeroid mice.

**Key findings:**
- Identified six SCAP networks upregulated in senescent cells: ephrin/dependence receptors, PI3Kδ, p21/serpines, BCL-2/BCL-xL, HIF-1α, and metabolic networks.
- siRNA silencing of individual SCAP nodes selectively killed senescent cells without harming proliferating or quiescent cells.
- Dasatinib selectively killed senescent preadipocytes; quercetin selectively killed senescent HUVECs and BM-MSCs; the combination was effective against both cell types.
- In aged mice (24 months), a single dose of D+Q improved cardiac ejection fraction and carotid vascular reactivity within 5 days.
- In radiation-injured mice, a single dose of D+Q improved treadmill exercise capacity, an effect that persisted for at least 7 months.
- Intermittent D+Q administration in progeroid (Ercc1−/Δ) mice delayed age-related symptoms, reduced osteoporosis, preserved intervertebral disk proteoglycan content, and extended healthspan.

### 5.2 Justice et al. 2019 — First-in-Human Open-Label Pilot in IPF

**Citation:** Justice JN, Nambiar AM, Tchkonia T, LeBrasseur NK, Pascual R, Hashmi SK, Prata L, Masternak MM, Kritchevsky SB, Musi N, Kirkland JL. Senolytics in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: Results from a first-in-human, open-label, pilot study. *EBioMedicine*. 2019;40:554-563. doi:10.1016/j.ebiom.2018.12.052. PMID: 30616998.

**Design:** Open-label, single-arm pilot study in 14 patients with IPF (mild-to-moderate, stable on standard therapy). Dasatinib 100 mg/day + quercetin 1250 mg/day for 3 consecutive days per week for 3 weeks (9 dosing days total).

**Key findings:**
- 100% retention; 13 of 14 participants completed all planned assessments.
- Statistically significant improvement in physical function: 6-minute walk distance (mean +21.5 m), 4-meter gait speed, and chair-stand test time (all p < 0.05).
- Pulmonary function (FVC, DLCO) remained stable, consistent with disease stabilization.
- Most adverse events were mild-to-moderate (respiratory symptoms, skin bruising, GI discomfort). One serious adverse event reported (pneumonia), deemed unlikely related to treatment.
- Established feasibility and tolerability of intermittent D+Q dosing in a clinical population.

### 5.3 Hickson et al. 2019 — Proof-of-Concept in Diabetic Kidney Disease

**Citation:** Hickson LJ, Langhi Prata LGP, Bobart SA, Evans TK, Giorgadze N, Hashmi SK, et al. Senolytics decrease senescent cells in humans: Preliminary report from a clinical trial of Dasatinib plus Quercetin in individuals with diabetic kidney disease. *EBioMedicine*. 2019;47:446-456. doi:10.1016/j.ebiom.2019.08.069. PMID: 31542391.

**Design:** Open-label, single-center study in 9 patients with diabetic kidney disease. Dasatinib 100 mg + quercetin 1000 mg orally for 3 consecutive days.

**Key findings:**
- First demonstration that D+Q decreases senescent cell burden in living humans.
- Adipose tissue senescent cells (SA-β-gal+), p16^INK4a+ cells, and p21^CIP1+ cells were significantly reduced within 11 days of a single 3-day course.
- Circulating SASP factors (IL-1α, IL-6, MMP-9, MMP-12) were significantly reduced.
- Skin epidermal p16^INK4a+ cell abundance decreased.
- Adipose tissue macrophage infiltration (crown-like structures) was reduced.
- No clinically significant adverse events. Kidney function (eGFR) remained stable.

- **US11517572B2 (Granted Patent):** Same family. Granted December 6, 2022. Anticipated expiration ~2035. The U.S. government retains certain rights under NIH Grant Nos. AG41122 and AG046061.

### 6.2 Unity Biotechnology Filings

Unity Biotechnology, Inc. was co-assignee on the original PCT filing (WO2015116740A1) and has filed separate patent families covering alternative senolytic modalities:

- **UBX0101:** p53/MDM2 interaction inhibitor for osteoarthritis (reached Phase 2; subsequently out-licensed).
- **UBX1325:** BCL-xL inhibitor for age-related ophthalmic conditions (diabetic macular edema, age-related macular degeneration).
- **WO2020092117A2:** "Killing senescent cells and treating senescence-associated diseases or disorders using a combination of a BCL inhibitor and an MCL-1 inhibitor." Covers BCL family-targeted senolytics distinct from the D+Q mechanism.
- Unity's broader patent portfolio (US12221433, US11980616, US10517866, US10478432) covers acyl sulfonamide-based BCL antagonists and methods for selectively eliminating senescent cells across multiple disease areas.

### 6.3 Relevance to This Disclosure

The granted Mayo/Kirkland claims (US11517572B2) are narrow: parenteral dasatinib plus quercetin for lessening idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis symptoms; the broader Src-inhibitor-plus-flavonoid application in the same family (US20170216286A1) is listed as abandoned. These claims have a 2014 priority date and are anticipated to expire around 2035. This defensive disclosure documents the publicly available evidence base, published dosing protocols, and known mechanisms such that no subsequent party may obtain method-of-use patents on variations, combinations, or new indications of the D+Q regimen that are obvious extensions of the existing published art. Specifically, any future filing claiming D+Q at substantially similar doses and schedules for senescence-associated conditions not explicitly covered in the existing claims should be anticipated by this disclosure in combination with the cited published literature.

## 8. Limitations and Disclaimer

1. **Not medical advice.** This disclosure describes published scientific findings and is not a recommendation for treatment. Dasatinib is a prescription medication with significant side effects including myelosuppression, pleural effusion, pulmonary arterial hypertension, QT prolongation, and hemorrhage. Any clinical use requires physician supervision.
2. **Patent scope uncertainty.** While existing Mayo/Kirkland patents cover the D+Q combination for senescence-associated conditions, the precise claim scope, continuation filings, and international prosecution status may evolve. This disclosure does not constitute a freedom-to-operate opinion.
3. **Limited clinical evidence.** The published human trials cited here are pilot-scale (9–14 patients), open-label or single-blind, and short-duration. Phase 2/3 randomized controlled trials are needed to establish efficacy, optimal dosing, and long-term safety. The 2023 placebo-controlled follow-up by Nambiar et al. (EBioMedicine, 2023;90:104481) randomized only 12 patients; there were no serious adverse events related to D+Q, but non-serious adverse events were more frequent in the D+Q arm (65 vs 22), with sleep disturbances and anxiety reported in 4/6 D+Q vs 0/6 placebo participants.
4. **Regulatory status.** Dasatinib is FDA-approved only for CML and Ph+ ALL. Its use as a senolytic is investigational and off-label. Quercetin is available as a dietary supplement but has not been evaluated by the FDA for senolytic indications.
5. **Bioavailability.** Quercetin has notoriously poor oral bioavailability (~2% in unformulated powder). Published trials used specific formulations; bioequivalence across commercial quercetin supplements is not guaranteed.
6. **Combination effects.** Long-term effects of intermittent D+Q on non-senescent cells, immune function, stem cell populations, and tumor surveillance remain under investigation. Removal of senescent cells may have context-dependent consequences (e.g., wound healing impairment, tumor promotion in certain microenvironments).
