## 1. Compound Identification

**Compound:** Sulforaphane (1-isothiocyanato-4-methylsulfinylbutane)

**CAS Registry Number:** 4478-93-7

**Molecular Formula:** C₆H₁₁NOS₂

**Molecular Weight:** 177.29 g/mol

**IUPAC Name:** 1-isothiocyanato-4-(methylsulfinyl)butane

**Chemical Class:** Isothiocyanate; produced by myrosinase-catalyzed hydrolysis of glucoraphanin (4-methylsulfinylbutyl glucosinolate), the predominant glucosinolate in broccoli (*Brassica oleracea* var. *italica*) and especially concentrated in broccoli sprouts (3-day-old seedlings contain 20–50× the glucoraphanin of mature broccoli florets).

**Natural Occurrence:** Found in cruciferous vegetables including broccoli, broccoli sprouts, Brussels sprouts, cabbage, cauliflower, kale, and watercress. Broccoli sprouts are the richest characterized natural source. The precursor glucoraphanin is stable in the plant matrix; conversion to sulforaphane occurs upon tissue damage (chewing, cutting) via endogenous myrosinase or by gut microbiota thioglucosidases during digestion.

**Regulatory / Supplement Status:** Sulforaphane and broccoli sprout extracts standardized to glucoraphanin or sulforaphane content are sold commercially in the United States, European Union, and elsewhere as dietary supplements under frameworks including the Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act of 1994 (DSHEA, U.S.) and the EU Food Supplements Directive (2002/46/EC). They are not classified as drugs by the FDA. Multiple brands market broccoli sprout extract supplements standardized to deliver defined sulforaphane or glucoraphanin yields (e.g., Avmacol, BroccoMax, Prostaphane). Sulforaphane has been assigned Generally Recognized as Safe (GRAS) status in multiple food-matrix contexts.

## 2. Therapeutic Application

**Condition:** Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) — a neurodevelopmental condition characterized by deficits in social communication and interaction, restricted and repetitive behaviors, and sensory processing differences. ASD is diagnosed under DSM-5 criteria (299.00; F84.0 under ICD-10) and affects approximately 1 in 36 children in the United States (CDC ADDM Network, 2023 report on 2020 surveillance data).

**Targeted Symptom Domains:**

- **Social responsiveness** — measured by the Social Responsiveness Scale (SRS), which quantifies severity of social impairment in naturalistic social settings, covering social awareness, social cognition, social communication, social motivation, and restricted interests/repetitive behavior.
- **Aberrant behavior** — measured by the Aberrant Behavior Checklist (ABC), which assesses irritability/agitation, lethargy/social withdrawal, stereotypic behavior, hyperactivity/noncompliance, and inappropriate speech.
- **Verbal communication** — assessed through the Clinical Global Impression–Improvement (CGI-I) scale, capturing clinician-rated changes in social interaction, aberrant behavior, and verbal communication domains.

**Therapeutic Rationale:** ASD is associated with well-documented biochemical abnormalities including elevated oxidative stress, depressed intracellular glutathione levels, mitochondrial dysfunction, neuroinflammation (elevated cytokines including TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β in cerebrospinal fluid and peripheral blood), and heat shock protein dysregulation. These abnormalities are not universal across all individuals with ASD but have been documented in substantial subpopulations across multiple independent studies. Sulforaphane addresses these abnormalities through Nrf2-mediated upregulation of antioxidant and cytoprotective gene networks (detailed in Section 4).

**Observation from Fever Response:** The clinical rationale for testing sulforaphane in ASD was partly motivated by the well-documented "fever effect" in autism, in which a substantial minority of individuals with ASD show transient behavioral improvement during febrile episodes. Fever induces heat shock protein expression and cellular stress responses that overlap with the cytoprotective pathways activated by sulforaphane, suggesting a common mechanistic link.

## 3. Proposed Protocol

This section describes the therapeutic protocol as implemented in the landmark Singh et al. (2014) randomized controlled trial, supplemented by subsequent trial protocols that varied dosing and population parameters.

**Route of Administration:** Oral

**Formulation:** Broccoli sprout extract standardized to deliver a quantified daily dose of sulforaphane. In the Singh et al. (2014) trial, the extract was produced under GMP conditions, providing a defined sulforaphane yield upon myrosinase-catalyzed conversion. Commercial supplements standardized to glucoraphanin content (with or without exogenous myrosinase) represent equivalent delivery vehicles.

**Dosing:**

| Body Weight | Daily Sulforaphane Dose |
|---|---|
| < 100 lbs (< 45 kg) | ~50 µmol/day |
| 100–199 lbs (45–90 kg) | ~100 µmol/day |
| ≥ 200 lbs (≥ 90 kg) | ~150 µmol/day |

These weight-based tiers were used in Singh et al. (2014). The dose range of 50–150 µmol/day (~9–27 mg sulforaphane/day) is consistent across published trials and falls within the range achievable from commercially available broccoli sprout extract supplements.

**Duration:** 18 weeks of daily administration in the Singh et al. (2014) trial, followed by a 4-week washout observation period. The 2022 multi-center Chinese trial (Ou et al.) used a 12-week course. The 2021 Zimmerman et al. trial used a 15-week double-blind course.

**Population:** Individuals with moderate-to-severe ASD. Singh et al. (2014) enrolled males aged 13–27 years. Subsequent trials expanded to children aged 3–12 years (Bent et al., 2018; Zimmerman et al., 2021) and included both sexes.

**Monitoring:** Serial administration of the ABC, SRS, and CGI-I at baseline and at regular intervals (4, 10, and 18 weeks in Singh et al. 2014). Biomarker panels (glutathione redox status, cytokine panels, heat shock protein levels, mitochondrial respiration indices) can be collected from peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) for mechanistic monitoring.

**Safety:** Across all published trials, sulforaphane at these doses has shown a favorable safety profile. Reported adverse events include mild gastrointestinal effects (flatulence, constipation, loose stools) and weight gain in some participants, generally not statistically different from placebo. No serious adverse events attributable to sulforaphane have been reported in the ASD trial literature.

## 4. Mechanism of Action

The behavioral improvements observed with sulforaphane administration in ASD are attributed to a cascade initiated by activation of the Keap1/Nrf2 cytoprotective signaling pathway, with convergent effects on oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, mitochondrial function, and cellular stress responses. The pathway proceeds as follows:

**Step 1 — Keap1 Modification:**
Sulforaphane is a potent electrophilic isothiocyanate. Upon systemic absorption, it covalently modifies reactive cysteine thiol residues on Kelch-like ECH-associated protein 1 (Keap1), particularly C151, C257, C273, C288, and C297 in the intervening region (IVR) between the BTB and Kelch repeat domains. Under basal conditions, Keap1 functions as a substrate adaptor for the Cul3-Rbx1 E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, targeting the transcription factor Nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (Nrf2) for polyubiquitination and constitutive proteasomal degradation.

**Step 2 — Nrf2 Stabilization and Nuclear Translocation:**
Covalent modification of Keap1 cysteine residues by sulforaphane disrupts the Keap1-Nrf2 protein-protein interaction, preventing Nrf2 ubiquitination. Newly synthesized Nrf2 is no longer degraded and accumulates in the cytoplasm, then translocates to the nucleus where it heterodimerizes with small Maf proteins.

**Step 3 — Antioxidant Response Element (ARE) Activation:**
The Nrf2-sMaf heterodimer binds to Antioxidant Response Elements (AREs; core sequence 5'-TGACnnnGC-3') in the promoter regions of over 200 cytoprotective genes, activating their transcription.

**Step 4 — Downstream Gene Induction:**

The ARE-driven transcriptional program includes:

- **Glutathione synthesis enzymes:** Glutamate-cysteine ligase catalytic subunit (GCLC) and modifier subunit (GCLM), glutathione synthetase (GSS) — increasing de novo glutathione (GSH) production, the cell's primary thiol antioxidant.
- **Glutathione recycling:** Glutathione reductase (GSR), which regenerates reduced GSH from oxidized GSSG.
- **Phase II detoxification enzymes:** Glutathione S-transferases (GSTs), NAD(P)H quinone dehydrogenase 1 (NQO1), UDP-glucuronosyltransferases (UGTs), heme oxygenase-1 (HMOX1).
- **Thioredoxin system:** Thioredoxin reductase 1 (TXNRD1), thioredoxin (TXN) — a parallel antioxidant electron-transfer system.
- **Anti-inflammatory mediators:** HMOX1 generates carbon monoxide (CO) and biliverdin, both of which have anti-inflammatory signaling functions.

**Step 5 — Oxidative Stress Reduction:**
Upregulation of GSH synthesis and recycling directly counteracts the elevated oxidative stress documented in ASD subpopulations. Multiple studies have reported lower GSH:GSSG ratios, elevated lipid peroxidation markers (8-isoprostane, malondialdehyde), and impaired total antioxidant capacity in individuals with ASD compared to neurotypical controls.

**Step 6 — Mitochondrial Function Improvement:**
Nrf2 activation improves mitochondrial bioenergetics through multiple pathways: GSH replenishment protects mitochondrial membranes from oxidative damage, NQO1 participates in mitochondrial electron transport, and Nrf2 can induce mitochondrial biogenesis factors. The Zimmerman et al. (2021) trial confirmed that sulforaphane treatment produced measurable changes in mitochondrial respiration parameters in PBMCs of children with ASD.

**Step 7 — Neuroinflammation Reduction:**
Nrf2 activation suppresses NF-κB-mediated pro-inflammatory signaling through multiple mechanisms: HMOX1-derived CO inhibits NF-κB nuclear translocation, elevated GSH dampens redox-sensitive NF-κB activation, and Nrf2 directly competes with NF-κB for the transcriptional co-activator CBP. In a BTBR T+tf/J mouse model of autism, sulforaphane ameliorated autism-like symptoms through suppression of Th17-related signaling and rectification of oxidant-antioxidant imbalance in both peripheral tissues and brain (Nadeem et al., 2019, Behavioural Brain Research).

**Step 8 — Heat Shock Protein (HSP) Induction:**
Sulforaphane independently activates heat shock factor 1 (HSF1) signaling, inducing expression of heat shock proteins including HSP27 and HSP70. HSP27 enhances proteasome activity and protein homeostasis. This pathway is particularly relevant to ASD given the documented "fever effect" — transient behavioral improvement during febrile episodes in a subpopulation of individuals with ASD, hypothesized to be mediated by fever-induced HSP expression. The Zimmerman et al. (2021) trial confirmed changes in heat shock protein biomarkers in sulforaphane-treated participants.

**Step 9 — Behavioral Improvement:**
The convergent effects of reduced oxidative stress, improved mitochondrial function, decreased neuroinflammation, and enhanced proteostasis collectively improve neuronal function and synaptic signaling. This manifests as measurable improvements in social responsiveness, reduced aberrant behavior, and improved verbal communication as quantified by standardized instruments (ABC, SRS, CGI-I). These improvements are reversible upon sulforaphane discontinuation, consistent with a pharmacological mechanism requiring sustained Nrf2 pathway activation rather than a permanent structural change.

## 5. Evidence Summary

### 5.1. Singh et al. (2014) — Landmark RCT

**Citation:** Singh K, Connors SL, Macklin EA, Smith KD, Fahey JW, Talalay P, Zimmerman AW. Sulforaphane treatment of autism spectrum disorder (ASD). *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*. 2014;111(43):15550–15555. doi:10.1073/pnas.1416940111. PMID: 25313065. PMCID: PMC4217462.

**Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial.

**Participants:** 44 young men (aged 13–27) with moderate-to-severe ASD. 29 received sulforaphane; 15 received placebo.

**Intervention:** Daily oral sulforaphane from broccoli sprout extract (50–150 µmol/day, weight-tiered) for 18 weeks, followed by 4-week washout.

**Results:**
- **Aberrant Behavior Checklist (ABC):** Sulforaphane group showed reductions of −20.2% at 4 weeks (P=0.035), −31.5% at 10 weeks (P=0.002), and −33.6% at 18 weeks (P<0.001) versus <3.3% change in placebo.
- **Social Responsiveness Scale (SRS):** Sulforaphane group showed −16.8% at 18 weeks (P=0.017) versus minimal change in placebo.
- **Clinical Global Impression–Improvement (CGI-I):** Significant improvement in social interaction (P=0.015), aberrant behavior (P=0.007), and verbal communication (P=0.015).
- **Reversibility:** Scores reverted toward pretreatment levels during the 4-week washout.
- **Safety:** Negligible toxicity. No serious adverse events.

### 5.2. Zimmerman et al. (2021) — Pediatric RCT

**Citation:** Zimmerman AW, Singh K, Connors SL, et al. Randomized controlled trial of sulforaphane and metabolite discovery in children with Autism Spectrum Disorder. *Molecular Autism*. 2021;12:38. doi:10.1186/s13229-021-00447-5. PMID: 34034808. PMCID: PMC8146218.

**Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial.

**Participants:** 57 children aged 3–12 with ASD.

**Results:** Non-statistically significant improvement on the primary outcome measure (Ohio Autism Clinical Impressions Scale). However, caregivers reported significant improvement on the ABC at 15 weeks versus placebo. Biomarker analyses demonstrated significant changes in glutathione redox status, mitochondrial respiration parameters, inflammatory markers, and heat shock protein levels in the sulforaphane group. Sulforaphane was very well tolerated with no serious side effects.

### 5.3. Ou et al. (2022) — Multi-Center RCT (China)

**Citation:** Ou J, Smith RC, Tobe RH, et al. Efficacy of Sulforaphane in Treatment of Children with Autism Spectrum Disorder: A Randomized Double-Blind Placebo-Controlled Multi-center Trial. *Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders*. 2024;54(2):628–641. doi:10.1007/s10803-022-05784-9.

**Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multi-center trial.

**Participants:** 108 children with ASD across multiple sites in China.

**Results:** No significant differences on caregiver-rated scales between groups. However, clinician-rated scales showed significant improvement in the sulforaphane group. One-third of sulforaphane participants showed ≥30% decrease in clinician-rated scores by 12 weeks. Effects were more pronounced in participants over 10 years of age. Treatment was safe and well-tolerated.

### 5.4. 2025 Meta-Analysis

**Citation:** Long J, Liao X, Tang Z, et al. Investigating the clinical efficacy, safety and molecular mechanism of sulforaphane in autism spectrum disorder: an integrated study combining meta-analysis, network pharmacology, and computational biology. *BMC Pharmacology and Toxicology*. 2025;26:217. doi:10.1186/s40360-025-01052-5.

**Design:** Meta-analysis of 6 randomized controlled trials (333 total participants) combined with network pharmacology and computational biology analyses.

**Results:** Pooled analysis demonstrated that both 4–5 weeks and 8–10 weeks of sulforaphane supplementation significantly decreased Social Responsiveness Scale scores compared to placebo. No significant differences in adverse event incidence between sulforaphane and placebo. Network pharmacology identified anti-oxidant and anti-inflammatory pathways as primary mechanisms, concluding that sulforaphane may serve as an "efficacious and safe adjunctive therapy for ASD."

### 5.5. Preclinical Confirmation

**Citation:** Nadeem A, Ahmad SF, Al-Harbi NO, et al. Nrf2 activator, sulforaphane ameliorates autism-like symptoms through suppression of Th17 related signaling and rectification of oxidant-antioxidant imbalance in periphery and brain of BTBR T+tf/J mice. *Behavioural Brain Research*. 2019;364:213–224. doi:10.1016/j.bbr.2019.02.031. PMID: 30790585.

The BTBR T+tf/J inbred mouse strain exhibits robust autism-like behavioral phenotypes (social deficits, repetitive behaviors). Sulforaphane administration ameliorated these behaviors through suppression of Th17-related signaling and restoration of oxidant-antioxidant balance in both peripheral tissues and brain, confirming the Nrf2-mediated mechanism in an animal model.

### 6.3. Implications for This Disclosure

The existence of these patents demonstrates active commercial interest in patent-protecting sulforaphane-based ASD therapies. This defensive disclosure establishes anticipatory prior art for:

- Any future method-of-use claims on sulforaphane or Nrf2-activating isothiocyanates for ASD behavioral management that are not already covered by the above patents.
- Dosing regimen variations (e.g., different weight tiers, durations, formulation standardization methods) not explicitly claimed in existing patents.
- Combination protocols (sulforaphane with behavioral therapy, other supplements, or monitoring biomarker panels).
- Use of sulforaphane for specific ASD behavioral sub-domains (e.g., specifically for verbal communication improvement, or specifically for reduction of repetitive behaviors) that may not be individually claimed.
- Application of other glucoraphanin-rich or sulforaphane-yielding plant extracts (moringa, watercress, red cabbage sprouts) to the same ASD therapeutic protocol.

This disclosure does not challenge the validity of existing patents. It aims to prevent future patent claims on extensions, variations, and applications that are obvious in light of the published evidence.

## 8. Limitations and Disclaimer

1. **Not medical advice.** This disclosure is a scientific and legal document intended to serve as prior art. It is not a recommendation for treatment. Individuals considering sulforaphane supplementation for ASD or any other condition should consult qualified healthcare providers.

2. **Heterogeneity of ASD.** ASD is a spectrum condition with highly variable etiology and presentation. Not all individuals with ASD exhibit the oxidative stress, glutathione depletion, or neuroinflammation phenotypes that the sulforaphane mechanism addresses. Published trial results show subgroup variability in response.

3. **Mixed clinical evidence.** While the Singh et al. (2014) trial showed statistically significant results on multiple outcome measures, subsequent trials have shown mixed results on primary endpoints, with some measures reaching significance and others not. The 2025 meta-analysis supports efficacy on the SRS across pooled data but acknowledges heterogeneity.

4. **Supplement variability.** Commercial broccoli sprout extracts vary in glucoraphanin content, myrosinase activity, sulforaphane yield, and bioavailability. Standardization is not uniform across manufacturers. Actual sulforaphane delivery from a given supplement may differ from the doses used in published trials.

5. **Regulatory status.** Sulforaphane supplements are marketed under DSHEA as dietary supplements. They have not been approved by the FDA for the treatment, cure, or prevention of any disease, including ASD.

6. **Existing patents.** This disclosure acknowledges the existence of active U.S. patents (Section 6) that may cover certain methods of using sulforaphane for ASD treatment. This disclosure does not constitute legal advice regarding freedom-to-operate under those patents.
